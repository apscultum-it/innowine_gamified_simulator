# INNOWINE D3.5 – VET Facilitator Guide (pilot)

**Target audience:** VET learners, trainers, producers, technicians and rural SMEs. **Languages currently implemented:** English and Italian. **Pedagogical approach:** scenario-based decision making, experiential learning, evidence-based reflection and comparison of learning gains.

## Learning outcomes

After completing the game, participants should be able to:

1. Evaluate dealcoholisation and quality decisions using technical evidence rather than assumed benefits.
2. Distinguish defensible EU no/low alcohol statements from misleading claims; identify where national/technical review is required.
3. Explain trade-offs among energy, water, materials, sensory outcomes, economics and circularity.
4. Design accessible digital product information while separating mandatory labelling from promotional storytelling.
5. Compare pricing, consumer segmentation, export and inclusive wine-tourism choices.
6. Reflect on errors, explain decisions and document practical improvements.

## Suggested 75-minute workshop

| Time | Activity | Facilitation prompts |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 min | Welcome, project, learning objectives and privacy notice | Explain that scoring is illustrative. |
| 10–20 min | Individual baseline: scenario 01 | Do not provide correct answers before assessment. |
| 20–40 min | Small groups: choose 3–4 specialist scenarios | Ask what evidence they would request from their real company. |
| 40–55 min | Role play: teams compare decisions | How would a producer, VET trainer and buyer disagree? |
| 55–65 min | Individual final: scenario 15 | The five domains mirror scenario 01. |
| 65–75 min | Export evidence, anonymous feedback and debrief | Review risky decisions and one workplace application. |

For a full independent training track, participants can complete **all 15 scenarios** (49 decisions) over multiple sessions. Trainers should not show feedback from scenario 15 before learners have finished it.

## Scoring rubric

| Grade | Meaning | Impact on error count |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Evidence-led, realistic and appropriately cautious | Not an error |
| 1 | Partial response / important trade-off still unresolved | Not an error; recorded as partial |
| 0 | Material avoidable risk, unsupported claim or inadequate control | **One error** |

Scores use 100 / 50 / 0 points per decision. Five abstract indicators (`quality`, `planet`, `trust`, `market`, `access`) rise or fall through illustrative changes; do not interpret them as emissions, CO₂e, real financial returns or product compliance probabilities.

**Before/after design:** scenarios 01 and 15 each contain five comparable decisions for (1) alcohol claims, (2) sustainability claims, (3) pricing, (4) QR compliance, (5) visitor inclusion. Individual change is relative to the baseline error rate, with zero baseline treated as not measurable. This is a formative proxy, not a validated psychometric test. For project reporting, pre-register cohort selection, review item equivalence, examine floor effects and report absolute error rates alongside the relative percentage.

## Evidence and ethical handling

The game exports a CSV choice log and a JSON report. Each event contains an anonymous-looking **pseudonymous** browser ID, attempt ID, scenario, question index, grade, skill, selected option, role and timestamp. The default browser-only mode supports a demonstration but **cannot** establish consortium-wide target attainment. A centrally operated telemetry service (see `DEPLOY_ANALYTICS.md`) can aggregate distinct completers and choices only after appropriate consents/legal basis and safeguards.

Never ask young learners to enter personal identifiers into screenshots or exported files. Keep keys or private data outside project repositories. The simulator has not been independently audited for WCAG conformance; include keyboard-only and assistive-technology users in the partner pilot.

## Pilot evaluation form (suggested)

Ask each pilot learner or trainer to rate on a 1–5 scale: instructions clarity, relevance to working life, perceived realism, technical accuracy, ease of use, accessibility, feedback usefulness, motivation to complete and confidence in applying the lessons. Add open fields for an unclear decision, an incorrect legal/technical assumption, and one change requested. Include a separate consent and demographics protocol as legally appropriate.

**Acceptance criteria for beta-to-final:** reviewer sign-off on all 49 prompts and 147 feedback explanations, successful use on mobile and desktop, export/restore checks, QA on 01/15 comparison, documentation of technical/source references, translations and consortium-grade usage reporting.
